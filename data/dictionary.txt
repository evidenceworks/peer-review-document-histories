DATA UNITS AND CONVENTIONS

The three eLife URL strata have the following reader-facing labels:
Generic URL = review_with_generic_url
DOI URL = review_with_DOI_url
No URL = review_no_url
The data and calculation criteria retain the codes on the right.

Binary fields use 1/0 or True/False, as encoded in each CSV; JSON uses
true/false. The reproduction program accepts 1 and true (case-insensitively)
as positive, and 0 and false as negative. Missing or unrecognized binary values
raise an error. These values indicate whether the criterion defined for that
field was met. An empty value retains its field-specific meaning. For display,
True/1 means Yes and False/0 means No.

elife_documents.csv: one distinct publisher output DOI per row (1,803 rows).
family is the related article-family identifier. role is review-article,
evaluation-summary or reply. step_id and step_rank retain the source-declared
activity and its order. published retains exact timestamp precision; input_dois
and input_versions describe the evaluated manuscript. endpoint, intermediary
and augmented are binary source-correspondence indicators. Multiple input
identifiers are separated by a vertical bar.

elife_endpoint_rows.csv: one eligible endpoint assertion (2,264 rows).
event_id and endpoint_record identify the assertion and bibliographic record.
input_doi and evaluation_time are the matching fields. candidate_count and
selected_source_doi record exact matching; an empty selected_source_doi is
abstention. Rows can identify the same source document repeatedly.

elife_families.csv: one sampled family (210 rows). sample_arm separates the
60 fixed earlier selections from the 150 additional random selections. stratum
identifies the fixed-frame URL class. Count and binary outcome fields are
independently reconstructed from the preceding document and row tables.

elife_census.csv: one source family (4,856 rows). has_vor and exact_MED select
the separate version-of-record discovery population. Route indicators and
residual_class describe captured bibliographic relations. first_vor_age_days
is source publication age at the census cutoff.

elife_residual.csv: the 647 census families without an enriched review event.
Upstream evidence, declared input-DOI availability and operational fallback
eligibility remain separate fields.

elife_assessments.csv and elife_publisher_api.csv: selected current-assessment
identifiers, source-reference agreement and abstention for their respective
3,550-family and 120-family populations.

elife_assessment_histories.csv: source-derived historical assessment comparisons
for 3,550 version-of-record families. Lower and upper fields preserve unresolved
source identity instead of assigning a preferred candidate.

bmc_documents.csv: one publicly linked PDF attachment (554 rows across 72
articles). doi, filename, role and stage identify the public source item.
bytes_equal records exact equality of the retrieved source PDF and the
corresponding decompressed archive member. source_url and archive_url identify
the sources. cohort separates 12 fixed articles from 60 additional random
selections. Preservation is defined at the article attachment-set level for inference. The public set
provides no measure of undisclosed editorial correspondence.

embo_families.csv: one EEB-attributed preprint (32 rows: two fixed and 30
additional). journal_group distinguishes EMBO journals from Life Science
Alliance. matching_event_rows counts qualifying assertions about composite
dossiers, rather than individual reports. event_present requires an
EMBO-attributed review event for the exact input DOI; event_with_url also
requires a nonempty URL, with all sampled preprints as its denominator.
direct_pointer_pdf denotes PDF bytes at a declared pointer;
pointer_homepage denotes a redirect to the publisher landing page.
source_pdf_recovered concerns the separate article route. A retrieved PDF can
contain a notice that restricts disclosure rather than the review exchange.

disclosure_cases.csv: three case-specific source-disclosure restrictions, with
article and preprint DOIs, source URLs and page numbers. This is a case table,
not a prevalence estimate for all journals or the complete service frame.

sampling.json: recorded frames, fixed observations, seeds and additional draws.
supporting_records.json: heading and content comparisons, source-identity
corrections and documentary relationships reported in Supplementary Information.

Counts are integers. Rates and confidence limits in generated outputs are
proportions; multiply by 100 for percentages or percentage-point differences.
An estimated range for eLife allows any outcomes in 11 unobserved small-stratum
families. Its separate 95% interval also incorporates the random draw from
2,829 remaining generic-URL families. Acquisition dates remain source provenance.

Exact confidence-interval inputs

Sampling!J24:J35 stores the positive counts used for the exact count limits in
columns D and E. Sampling!B56 and B57 retain the corresponding finite-population
size (2,829) and additional sample size (150). Columns H and I return #N/A if the
observed positive count, actual additional-row count, population size or stated
sample size no longer matches those inputs. Recalculate count limits with the
analysis script after changing the sampling inputs. The workbook represents the
fixed study data, rather than a general-purpose sampling calculator.

Reproduction boundary

The tables are derived observations, not historical XML/API responses or source
PDFs. The endpoint table includes eligible rows only, not a full exclusion
ledger. BMC byte-equality fields record the original comparisons; the archive
includes paired attachment digests and recorded inventory-match outcomes,
but not the independently enumerated raw inventories or PDF bytes. The selected text-comparison results and the full-sample exact-text fingerprints
are derived observations, not distributed full-text pairs. These limits matter when distinguishing
numerical reproducibility from independent source validation.

manifest.sha256 hashes the distributed derived-data files and this dictionary.
It detects changes to that snapshot; it does not authenticate historical source
acquisition. Generated statistics retain proportions on the 0–1 scale.

Recovered comparison records

bmc_documents.csv includes source_pdf_sha256, archive_pdf_sha256 and source_bytes
from the historical file comparisons. inventory_match records equality of the
independently enumerated publisher and archive filename sets for that article.
Preservation requires both inventory_match and bytes_equal for every attachment.
These fields retain the historical comparison evidence; they do not recreate
the source inventories or permit a new byte audit without the retained files.

elife_text_identity.csv contains one row per matched intermediary output with
text. source_record is the response basename; docmap_index, action_index and
output_index are zero-based JSON-array positions, and step_id is the intermediary
step key. source_doi identifies the matched publisher object, not a DOI asserted
by that intermediary output. Concatenate textual content blocks with a space,
collapse whitespace, trim, and case-fold before computing normalized_text_sha256.
normalized_characters is the resulting string length. Markup and punctuation
remain; this is an exact-string sensitivity, not semantic deduplication.

A stable text has one normalized string across all compatible observed outputs
for its source DOI. Duplicate groups are distinct source DOIs with the same
normalized string within a family. New-text checks compare recovered steps with
endpoint-matched objects, and later steps with earlier observed steps. Analysis
of wholly text-observed histories is restricted to those histories; it does not
estimate unique content in unreconstructed source objects.

The optional scripts/extract_text_identity.py program regenerates the text
fingerprints from separately supplied historical EEB response bodies. It does
not fetch current responses or redistribute the captured review texts.
